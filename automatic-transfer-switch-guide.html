What Is an Automatic Transfer Switch (ATS) — and Do You Need One?

When load-shedding hits at 2am, do you want someone to wake up, walk outside, start the generator and throw a changeover switch — or would you rather the power come back on its own within seconds? That is exactly what an automatic transfer switch (ATS) does. For businesses with cold rooms, clinics, security systems or servers, it is one of the most valuable upgrades you can make to a backup generator.

What an Automatic Transfer Switch Does

An automatic transfer switch sits between your ZESCO supply, your generator and your distribution board. It constantly monitors the mains supply. The moment it detects a power cut — or a dangerous drop in voltage — it follows a simple sequence:

  1. Waits a few seconds to make sure the outage is real and not a brief flicker.
  2. Sends a start signal to the generator.
  3. Once the generator is running at stable voltage and frequency, switches your load across to it.
  4. When ZESCO power returns and stays stable, switches the load back to mains.
  5. Lets the generator run for a cool-down period, then shuts it off.

The whole changeover typically takes 10 to 30 seconds, with nobody lifting a finger.

ATS vs Manual Changeover Switch

A manual changeover switch does the same job of switching between mains and generator, but a person has to start the generator and flip the switch by hand. It is cheaper and perfectly adequate for a home where someone is always around. The drawbacks are obvious: nobody is there at night, over weekends or during holidays, and people forget to switch back when ZESCO returns — leaving the generator burning fuel for hours.

An ATS costs more upfront and requires a generator with an electric start and auto-start controller, but it removes human error completely. For any business where an unattended outage means spoiled stock or lost trade, it quickly pays for itself.

Quick tip: Never connect a generator to your house by back-feeding through a socket with a "suicide lead". It can electrocute you, and it can send power back into ZESCO lines and kill a line worker. Always use a proper changeover switch or ATS.

Who Needs an ATS?

An automatic transfer switch is strongly recommended if you run:

  • Cold rooms, freezers and display fridges — butcheries, supermarkets, restaurants and farms where a few unattended hours can spoil thousands of kwacha of stock.
  • Clinics and pharmacies — where vaccines and medicines need an unbroken cold chain.
  • Security systems — electric fences, CCTV, gate motors and alarms that must never go down.
  • Offices and server rooms — often paired with a UPS to bridge the few seconds before the generator takes over.
  • Lodges, hotels and guest houses — where guests expect lights and water pumps to keep working.

Sizing and Choosing an ATS

An ATS must be rated for the full current of the circuits it switches, and for your supply type — single-phase for most homes, three-phase for most commercial premises (see single-phase vs three-phase power). Key things to look for:

  • Current rating matched to your main breaker and generator output, with headroom.
  • Mechanical and electrical interlocking so mains and generator can never be connected at the same time.
  • Adjustable timers for start delay, transfer delay and generator cool-down.
  • Voltage monitoring that also transfers on dangerous under- or over-voltage, not just a total blackout.
  • Phase monitoring on three-phase systems, to protect motors if one phase drops.

Your generator itself needs to be sized correctly too — our guide to choosing the right generator explains how.

Why Safe Changeover Matters

The single most important job of any changeover device is keeping the generator and the grid electrically separate. If both are connected at once, the generator can be destroyed when ZESCO power returns, and power can flow back up the lines — a lethal danger to anyone working on them. A properly installed ATS with interlocks makes this impossible. It also protects your equipment by only transferring once the generator's voltage and frequency are stable, preventing the brown-out damage that kills compressors and electronics.

Installation and Maintenance

An ATS installation should be done by a qualified electrician: it involves rewiring the incoming supply, integrating the generator's control panel, and often separating essential and non-essential circuits so the generator is not overloaded. Once installed, test it monthly by simulating an outage (switching off the mains isolator) and confirming the generator starts, takes the load and hands back cleanly. Include the ATS in your regular generator maintenance — contacts, timers and the generator's starting battery all need checking. Frequently Asked Questions

How fast does an automatic transfer switch restore power?

Typically 10 to 30 seconds, depending on the start delay setting and how quickly the generator reaches stable voltage. Pair it with a UPS if computers or servers cannot tolerate any interruption.

Can I add an ATS to my existing generator?

Yes, as long as the generator has an electric start. Many generators need an auto-start control module added. A technician will check compatibility during a site visit.

Is a manual changeover switch safe?

Yes. A correctly installed manual changeover switch is safe and legal. It simply relies on someone being present to start the generator and switch over.

Does an ATS work with solar and inverter systems?

Yes. An ATS can be configured so the inverter covers short outages and the generator starts automatically only when batteries run low, giving you a quiet, fuel-efficient backup setup.
