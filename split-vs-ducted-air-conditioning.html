Split vs Ducted Air Conditioning: Which Suits Your Building?

Fitting out a new office, clinic, shop or house? One of the biggest decisions is the type of air conditioning system. Wall-mounted split units are everywhere in Zambia, but ducted and cassette systems are increasingly popular in modern offices and upmarket homes. Each has clear strengths — and choosing wrongly can cost you in comfort, running costs and looks for years.

The Main Types of AC System

All of these systems work on the same principle: an outdoor unit containing the compressor, connected by refrigerant pipes to indoor equipment that cools the air. The difference is in how the cool air is delivered to your rooms:

  • Wall-mounted split — one indoor unit on the wall per room.
  • Multi-split — one outdoor unit serving two to five indoor units.
  • Cassette / ceiling-suspended — indoor units built into or hung from the ceiling, blowing air in several directions.
  • Ducted — a concealed indoor unit in the ceiling void, pushing cool air through ducts to discreet grilles in each room.

Wall-Mounted Split Units

Split units are the most common choice in Zambian homes and small offices, and for good reason. They are the cheapest to buy and install, each room is controlled independently, and if one unit fails the others keep working. Installation is quick and needs no ceiling work, and parts and technicians are widely available.

The downsides: every room needs its own outdoor unit (or a multi-split), which can clutter walls; the indoor units are visible; and in large open-plan areas, one or two wall units can leave hot and cold spots.

Cassette and Ceiling Units

Cassette units sit flush in a suspended ceiling and blow air in four directions, giving much more even cooling in larger rooms. They are a popular choice for open-plan offices, shops, banks, restaurants and waiting areas. They need a ceiling void and a condensate drain (usually with a built-in pump), so they cost more to install than wall splits. Ceiling-suspended units offer similar coverage where there is no suspended ceiling.

Ducted Air Conditioning

With a ducted system, all the equipment is hidden. A single indoor unit sits in the ceiling or a plant room and distributes air through insulated ductwork to slim grilles in each room, with a return air path back to the unit. The result is quiet, even, invisible cooling throughout the building, with fresh-air intake possible for better indoor air quality.

Ducted systems cost more upfront and are best planned during construction or a major renovation, when ceilings are open. Zoning dampers can control airflow to different areas, but individual room control is less flexible than with separate splits, and a fault in the single indoor unit affects the whole zone. For larger buildings, VRF systems combine many indoor units on a shared outdoor plant for maximum flexibility.

Quick tip: In Zambia, duct insulation is critical. Uninsulated or poorly sealed ducts running through a hot roof space can lose a large share of their cooling before the air ever reaches the room.

How to Choose

  • Existing home, few rooms, tight budget: wall-mounted inverter splits.
  • Open-plan office, shop or restaurant: cassette units, sized to the floor area and occupancy.
  • New build or major renovation, premium finish: ducted, planned with the architect from the start.
  • Large commercial building, many zones: ducted or VRF, designed by an HVAC engineer.

Whichever you choose, capacity must be calculated for the space. Our AC sizing guide explains the basics, and choosing inverter technology cuts running costs across every system type.

Planning and Installation

The best results come from involving an HVAC contractor early — ideally at design stage — to plan outdoor unit positions, pipe and duct routes, condensate drainage, electrical supply and access for maintenance. Commercial installations may need a three-phase supply. Once installed, every system needs regular servicing: filters, coils, drains and, for ducted systems, periodic duct inspection. Our air conditioning and engineering teams design and install split, cassette and ducted systems for homes and commercial projects across Zambia.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is ducted air conditioning more expensive to run than split units?

Not necessarily. A well-designed, well-insulated ducted inverter system can be as efficient as multiple splits. However, cooling the whole house when only one room is used wastes energy, so zoning is important.

Can I install ducted AC in an existing house?

Often yes, if there is enough roof or ceiling space for the indoor unit and ducts. It is more disruptive and costly than fitting splits, so a site survey is essential.

What is the best AC for an open-plan office?

Cassette units are usually the best balance of cost, even air distribution and appearance for open-plan offices. Larger or multi-floor offices may suit ducted or VRF systems.

How many split units can one outdoor unit run?

A multi-split outdoor unit typically serves two to five indoor units, depending on the model and its total capacity.
