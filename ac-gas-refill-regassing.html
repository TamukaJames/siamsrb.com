AC Gas Refill (Regassing): What Every Zambian AC Owner Should Know

"It just needs gas" is one of the most common things Zambian AC owners hear when their unit stops cooling. Sometimes it is true. But air conditioners do not use up refrigerant the way a car uses fuel — if the gas is low, it has leaked out somewhere. Understanding this can save you from paying for the same top-up again and again.

What AC "Gas" Actually Is

The "gas" in your air conditioner is a refrigerant — a special fluid that circulates in a sealed loop between the indoor and outdoor units. It absorbs heat from your room as it evaporates in the indoor coil, then releases that heat outside as the compressor squeezes it back into a liquid. Because the system is sealed, the same refrigerant should circulate for the whole life of the unit. A correctly installed, leak-free AC should never need a gas top-up.

Signs Your AC Is Low on Refrigerant

  • The unit runs but blows cool rather than cold air, and struggles on hot afternoons.
  • Ice or frost forms on the indoor coil or on the thin copper pipe at the outdoor unit.
  • A hissing or bubbling sound near the pipes or indoor unit.
  • The AC runs for much longer than it used to and your electricity bill rises.
  • Oily stains on pipe joints or flare connections — refrigerant carries compressor oil with it as it escapes.

These symptoms can also be caused by dirty filters, a blocked coil or a failing fan, so check our guide on why your AC isn't cooling before assuming it is the gas.

Why Gas Leaks Happen

Most leaks we find in Zambia come from a handful of causes:

  • Poor flare joints at installation — the most common cause by far. Badly cut or under-tightened flares slowly weep refrigerant.
  • Vibration rubbing pipes against walls, brackets or each other until a hole wears through.
  • Corrosion of the coil, especially near mining and industrial sites on the Copperbelt, around kitchen extractors, or where harsh coil-cleaning chemicals are used carelessly.
  • Physical damage from building work, burglar bars or careless handling.
  • Worn service valves and Schrader cores.

Quick tip: Be wary of any technician who offers to "top up" your AC without testing for leaks first. If there is a leak, the gas will be gone again in weeks or months — and you will be paying again.

R22, R410A and R32 Explained

Different air conditioners use different refrigerants, and they must never be mixed:

  • R22: found in older units. It damages the ozone layer and is being phased out worldwide, so it is increasingly scarce and expensive. If an old R22 unit has a major leak, replacement is often the smarter choice.
  • R410A: the standard in many split units from the last 15 years. It runs at higher pressure than R22 and must be charged as a liquid.
  • R32: common in newer, efficient inverter air conditioners. It has a lower environmental impact and good efficiency, but is mildly flammable, so it must be handled by trained technicians with the right equipment.

The refrigerant type and charge weight are printed on the outdoor unit's nameplate.

What a Proper Regas Involves

A professional refrigerant repair follows these steps:

  1. Diagnose — measure pressures and temperatures to confirm the system really is undercharged.
  2. Find the leak — using electronic leak detectors, bubble solution or nitrogen pressure testing.
  3. Repair — re-make flares, braze joints or replace damaged sections.
  4. Pressure test with dry nitrogen to confirm the repair holds.
  5. Vacuum the system to remove air and moisture, which otherwise damage the compressor.
  6. Charge by weight — adding the exact amount specified on the nameplate, not guessing by pressure alone.

Why Repeat Top-Ups Cost More

A quick top-up looks cheap, but running an AC low on refrigerant makes the compressor work harder and hotter, pushes up your power bill, and can eventually burn the compressor out — the most expensive part of the unit. Paying once for a proper leak repair almost always works out cheaper than paying for gas every season.

Preventing Leaks

The best protection is a quality installation with properly made flares and well-supported pipework, followed by regular servicing where a technician checks pressures, inspects joints and cleans the coils. For businesses running several units, a maintenance contract catches slow leaks before they become breakdowns. Our AC technicians carry leak detection and recovery equipment for all common refrigerants.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often does an AC need a gas refill?

A properly installed air conditioner should never need a gas refill. Refrigerant circulates in a sealed loop. If your AC needs regular top-ups, it has a leak that should be found and repaired.

Can I mix R22 and R410A?

No. Refrigerants must never be mixed. Each system is designed for one specific refrigerant, and mixing them damages the compressor and makes the system impossible to service properly.

How long does an AC regas take?

A simple recharge after a confirmed repair usually takes one to two hours. Finding and fixing a leak, pressure testing and vacuuming can take half a day, depending on where the leak is.

Is low refrigerant dangerous?

Modern refrigerants are not toxic in small amounts, but running an AC low on gas damages the compressor. R32 is mildly flammable, so leaks should be handled by trained technicians.
