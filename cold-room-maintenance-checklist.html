Cold Room Maintenance Checklist: Daily, Monthly and Yearly Tasks

A cold room is often the most valuable piece of equipment in a butchery, supermarket, restaurant, pharmacy or farm — and the stock inside it is worth even more. Most cold room breakdowns do not happen out of the blue; they build up over weeks through iced-up coils, dirty condensers and worn door seals. This checklist shows what your staff should check every day, and what your technician should handle every month and year.

Why a Checklist Matters

Zambia is tough on refrigeration. Hot afternoons push condensers to their limit, dry-season dust clogs coils, and load-shedding causes repeated hard restarts and voltage spikes. A cold room that is simply switched on and forgotten will slowly lose efficiency until one day it cannot hold temperature at all. A written checklist, with named staff responsible and a simple log sheet on the door, turns maintenance into routine and gives you an early warning long before stock is at risk.

Daily Checks (Staff)

  • Record the temperature at opening and closing — chillers typically 0°C to +4°C, freezer rooms −18°C to −22°C. Use the display and an independent thermometer.
  • Check the door closes and seals fully every time. Look out for pallets, crates or plastic curtains blocking it.
  • Look for ice building up on the evaporator (the fan unit inside), floor or door frame.
  • Listen for unusual noises — rattling fans, a compressor cycling on and off rapidly, or grinding.
  • Keep airflow clear — stock should not be stacked against the evaporator or blocking the fans. Leave space between shelves and walls.

Quick tip: A temperature log is also your evidence for health inspectors, insurers and — for pharmacies — regulators. Keep it on a clipboard on the cold room door and review it weekly.

Weekly Checks (Staff)

  • Clean floors, walls and shelves with a food-safe cleaner, and wipe up spills before they freeze.
  • Inspect door gaskets for tears, cracks or gaps. A simple test: close the door on a sheet of paper — if it slides out easily, the seal is leaking.
  • Check door heaters on freezer rooms — frost around the frame means the heater strip may have failed.
  • Clear the drain line area and check water isn't pooling under the evaporator.
  • Rotate stock on a first-in, first-out basis and remove anything expired.

Monthly Checks

  • Clean the condenser (outdoor unit) — brush off dust, leaves and debris, and make sure nothing blocks airflow around it. In the dry season, this may be needed every two weeks.
  • Check defrost cycles are working and the evaporator is clearing fully between defrosts.
  • Inspect door hinges, closers and handles, including the inside safety release.
  • Test alarms — high-temperature alarms and the "person trapped" alarm on walk-in freezers.
  • Test your backup power — confirm the generator or automatic transfer switch takes over when mains fails.

Quarterly Professional Service

Every three months, a refrigeration technician should:

  • Deep-clean condenser and evaporator coils with the correct coil cleaner.
  • Check refrigerant pressures, superheat and subcooling to spot slow leaks early.
  • Check compressor running current and voltage, and inspect contactors and relays for burning.
  • Clean and flush the condensate drain line and check the drain heater on freezer rooms.
  • Inspect fan motors and blades, and lubricate where required.
  • Calibrate the controller and temperature probes.

Annual Service

Once a year, go deeper: a full electrical inspection of the control panel and wiring, tightening of all terminals, checks on surge and phase protection, inspection of insulated panels and joints for damage or ice build-up (a sign of moisture getting into the insulation), a check on floor heating for freezer rooms, and a review of the year's temperature logs and fault history. This is also a good time to consider upgrades — remote temperature monitoring with SMS alerts, or a more efficient condensing unit.

Warning Signs to Act on Immediately

Call a technician straight away if you notice any of these:

  • Temperature rising and not recovering after the door has been closed for a while.
  • The evaporator coil is a solid block of ice.
  • The compressor is running constantly, or short-cycling every few minutes.
  • Water leaking inside the room or from the ceiling panels.
  • A burning smell, tripping breakers or scorched wiring at the control panel.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should a cold room be serviced?

We recommend a professional service every three months for most commercial cold rooms in Zambia, with daily and weekly checks carried out by your own staff. High-use or dusty sites may need monthly visits.

Why is my cold room evaporator icing up?

Common causes are a failed defrost heater or timer, a door left open or poorly sealed, blocked airflow from stacked stock, low refrigerant, or a faulty evaporator fan.

What temperature should a cold room be?

Chiller cold rooms for fresh produce, dairy and drinks typically run between 0 and +4 degrees Celsius. Freezer rooms for meat and frozen goods run between -18 and -22 degrees Celsius.

How do I protect my cold room during load-shedding?

Keep the door closed during outages, install a generator or inverter backup with an automatic transfer switch, fit surge and phase protection, and use a time delay so the compressor does not restart the instant power returns.
